Who else makes AI molecules you can actually make?

Competitive landscape for SyntheMol in small-molecule generative chemistry: {{N}} competitors and substitutes, their funding, how they price, and where SyntheMol sits.

SyntheMol = purchasable building blocks (~30B-molecule space in 2024, ~46B in 2026) + known reactions + activity model → molecule and its synthesis recipe

Bottom line

  1. "Synthesizable by design" is no longer unique. Iktos, PostEra, Onepot, Chemify and XtalPi all sell it, and several academic models (SynFormer, RxnFlow, SynFlowNet, ReaSyn) use the same building-block-plus-reaction idea. SyntheMol's own code is public. The pitch has to move from the method to the proof.
  2. SyntheMol's proof is strong for its size. Two peer-reviewed rounds with wet-lab hits: 6 novel A. baumannii actives from 58 made (2024), then 13 potent from 79 made with 7 novel and in vivo MRSA efficacy for synthecin (2026). Few direct competitors publish hit rates plus animal data for generated molecules.
  3. Money is in programs, not software. Insilico sold only $4.9M of software in 2025 against $56M total revenue. Schrödinger ($199.5M software) is the exception after 30 years. Platform deals pay $12–115M upfront, usually 2–5% of the headline value.
  4. Capital gap is extreme. Stoked closed a $2.5M seed. Isomorphic has ~$2.7B, Lila $550M, Genesis $400M+. SyntheMol cannot win as a horizontal platform. It can win a niche.
  5. Model access is being given away. NVIDIA GenMol, Boltz-2 (MIT licence), REINVENT 4 and Lilly TuneLab are free. Tamarind hosts 200+ models without code. Charging for "access to a model" will be hard.
  6. The best-defended position is biology-first, low-data, anti-infective design. The only focused rival is Phare Bio, a grant-funded non-profit. Stokes-lab phenotypic data plus validated in vivo hits is the moat to argue (hand-off to Sithum, WP-H).

Where SyntheMol can win

  • Every molecule ships with a recipe from purchasable blocks, so a chemist can order it right away (~3–4 weeks, >80% success at Enamine).
  • Works with small, phenotypic datasets (whole-cell growth inhibition), where structure-based tools like Isomorphic's or Boltz have no target structure to use.
  • Published, repeated wet-lab and in vivo validation in antibiotics, an area big AI players avoid.
  • Multi-objective RL (activity + solubility) in the 2026 version.

Where it is exposed

  • Public code plus a crowded academic field make the algorithm easy to copy.
  • Depends on Enamine's space. Onepot and Chemify own their "make" step.
  • Chemistry limited to a fixed set of reactions (13 in the 2024 paper), so scaffolds are less diverse than free-form generators.
  • Antibiotic market economics are weak, so buyers are few and pay little.

Four points to say in the teleconference.

Competitive intelligence · Generative chemistry · Dhruv

Synthesizability is now table stakes. Validated biology is the edge.

  • Direct rivals: Iktos (Servier €1B+ deal, 2026), PostEra (Pfizer AI Lab), Onepot (3.4B-molecule space + own lab), Chemify, Insilico Chemistry42, XtalPi.
  • Capital gap: Stoked $2.5M seed vs Isomorphic ~$2.7B, Genesis $400M+, Chemify ~$145M.
  • Pricing signal: software alone earns little (Insilico: $4.9M of $56M); value is in partnered programs with $12–115M upfronts.
  • SyntheMol's angle: recipe-included designs, low-data phenotypic models, and in vivo antibiotic proof (synthecin vs MRSA). Nearest niche rival: Phare Bio (non-profit, $27M ARPA-H).

Open questions for Stoked

  1. Who owns SyntheMol IP? The first paper came out of Stanford (Swanson, Zou) with McMaster (Stokes). Does Stoked hold an exclusive licence, and what licence covers the public GitHub code?
  2. Is SyntheMol-RL (2026) code public too, and is there an unreleased internal version?
  3. Which proprietary datasets (phenotypic screens, in vivo results) come with SyntheMol if it is spun out or licensed?
  4. Does Stoked have a relationship with Enamine or Chemspace (supply terms, co-marketing)?
  5. Target buyer: pharma anti-infective groups, AMR-focused biotechs, or non-profits and funders (CARB-X, ARPA-H, Wellcome)?

Still unverified: Schrödinger and Chemistry42 prices (third-party estimates only), Enamine per-compound cost (one forum estimate), Model Medicines funding (not found). Map positions and threat ratings are our own judgement.
